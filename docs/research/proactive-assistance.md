# Proactive AI assistance: practices from productivity thinkers (2026-10-18)

Goal: decide which proactive behaviours the web OS assistant should have, grounded in proven
personal-productivity methods rather than invented features. Each practice → what the assistant
does → where it lives in `web-os-v1`.

| Thinker / method | Core idea | What the assistant does |
|---|---|---|
| **David Allen — Getting Things Done** | Capture everything; clarify each input into a *next action*; keep "open loops" out of your head; weekly review; "waiting for" list; two-minute rule | Open loops (needs-reply, I owe, owed to me); every triaged mail gets a next action; two-minute items batched as "do now"; nudge drafts for overdue "waiting for"; Friday weekly review |
| **Stephen Covey — 7 Habits** | Personal mission statement; roles; "big rocks" first; Quadrant II (important, not urgent) | Personal constitution as the mission statement; weekly plan proposes big rocks per area (Personal, Digisoft, Quokka, RDIM) before smaller tasks |
| **Eisenhower matrix** | Urgent × important decides do / schedule / delegate / drop | Mail and loop priority = importance (person, goal link, money, deadline) × urgency (deadline, waiting time), with the reason shown |
| **Cal Newport — Deep Work, Slow Productivity** | Protect long focus blocks; batch shallow work; do fewer things | Mail revision only at fixed passes (batching); morning brief shows today's free focus blocks and suggests the deep task; warns when meeting load is high |
| **Greg McKeown — Essentialism** | Say no to the non-essential, gracefully | Weekly plan flags commitments that serve no goal and offers decline/defer drafts |
| **Andy Grove — High Output Management** | Managerial leverage; meetings must produce decisions and owners; delegate by task-relevant maturity | Meeting prep (attendees, history, open loops, agenda); after the meeting: decisions + action items → ClickUp task drafts for the team; delegation suggestions |
| **John Doerr / Grove — OKRs** | Objectives with measurable key results, reviewed regularly | Goals extracted from the 2026 personal and business plans (owner confirms); weekly review shows activity against each goal |
| **Peter Drucker — The Effective Executive** | "Know thy time": measure where time actually goes | Weekly attention audit: messages and meetings per area vs. the goal priorities |
| **Tiago Forte — Building a Second Brain (CODE, PARA)** | Capture, Organize (Projects/Areas/Resources/Archives), Distill, Express | Personal base marked by area/project; summaries = distill; drafts and plans = express |
| **Merlin Mann — Inbox Zero** | Process mail into actions (delete, delegate, respond, defer, do) quickly | Mail revision classes + one-tap actions; sender noise learning |
| **Annie Duke — decision journal** | Record decisions with context to review outcomes later | Decisions extracted from conversations and meetings, listed per scope and in the weekly review |

## Guardrails (from the owner's constraints)
- **Propose, never act alone:** drafts and task drafts wait for one-tap approval; nothing is sent
  automatically; Telegram stays read-only.
- **Every suggestion shows why and links its sources.**
- **Learn from corrections:** edits and discards tune later suggestions (tone examples, sender
  preferences, priority weights).
- **Budget:** drafts only for the top-priority items (default 10/day).

## Not adopted (for now)
- Automatic calendar time-blocking (needs calendar write access; show suggestions only).
- Habit tracking (Atomic Habits): no data source yet; can come from the personal base later.
- Monthly/quarterly reviews (GTD horizons of focus): possible extension of weekly routines.

## Sources (books)
Allen, *Getting Things Done* (2015 ed.); Covey, *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*; Newport,
*Deep Work* (2016) and *Slow Productivity* (2024); McKeown, *Essentialism* (2014); Grove, *High
Output Management* (1983); Doerr, *Measure What Matters* (2018); Drucker, *The Effective Executive*
(1967); Forte, *Building a Second Brain* (2022); Mann, "Inbox Zero" (43folders, 2006); Duke,
*Thinking in Bets* (2018).
